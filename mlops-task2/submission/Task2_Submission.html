4. Notebook 3 — why the split is chronological

This is the criterion the task asks to be explained. The notebook answers it with a measurement rather than an argument.

The usual argument does not apply here

Random splits are normally rejected because they leak customer history across the boundary. That was checked first:

EVIDENCE 6 — repeat customers
Distinct customers        : 93,350
Ordered more than once    : 2,801  (3.00%)
Ordered exactly once      : 90,549  (97.00%)

97% of customers ordered once. There is almost no customer history to leak, so this argument is weak in this dataset. The real reason lies elsewhere.

The real reason, as a number

The late rate is not stable over time — it ranges from under 1% to nearly 19% depending on the month. So the notebook measures how much signal the calendar alone carries: it uses the late rate of an order's own month as if it were a prediction, and scores it.

EVIDENCE 7 — the month, used as a predictor
ROC-AUC using the month's late rate alone as the prediction: 0.698

Knowing only the month scores 0.698 — higher than the purchase-time baseline model measured in Task 1 (0.679). A random split places March 2018 (19% late) and June 2018 (1% late) in train and test simultaneously, letting the model memorise month-level base rates. That information does not exist in production, where the model predicts for a month that has not happened yet.

The split, and the boundaries

SplitWindowOrdersShareLate
trainup to 2018-03-3164,32066.7%7.95%
validation2018-04-01 → 2018-05-3113,54714.0%5.53%
testfrom 2018-06-0118,60319.3%3.61%

Three candidate cut points were compared before choosing. An earlier validation window would have given it a late rate roughly triple the test set's, making it a misleading proxy for exactly the thing it exists to predict. The chosen boundaries keep validation and test adjacent in time.

The differing late rates are deliberate. The task asks to preserve the label ratio across splits if splitting randomly; this split is chronological, so the rates are left alone. The shift from 7.95% to 3.61% is the phenomenon the model has to face honestly, not a defect to be corrected.

The split is by purchase date, but a label is only known at delivery. 3,852 training orders (5.99%) were delivered after the validation window opened. The strict remedy — an embargo — would cost 6% of the training data for a small theoretical gain, so those rows were kept and the limitation is disclosed rather than hidden.

6. Notebook 5 — features, and the objects that must be saved

31 features. The discipline this step enforces: every statistic is learned from the training split alone, and everything learned is saved. In production a single order arrives — a median cannot be computed from it.

Target encoding without leaking the label

Replacing a state with its historical late rate introduces two hazards, both handled explicitly:

  • Small categories produce noise. A state with 12 orders and 2 late reads 16.7%. Smoothing pulls each estimate toward the overall rate with a strength of 50 pseudo-observations, so small categories barely move from the prior while large ones keep their own rate.
  • Rows would otherwise see their own label. Encoding a training row with a mean computed from the training set includes that row's label in its own feature. Inside train the encoding is therefore computed out of fold — 5 folds, each encoded from the other four. Validation and test use the full-train mapping, which is exactly what production will use.
EVIDENCE 11 — verified reproducibility, not claimed reproducibility
OK  31 features, no leakage, no gaps, identical structure across splits
OK  rebuilding from the loaded objects matches the notebook output exactly
    -> the pipeline is genuinely reproducible, not just claimed to be

The final cell reloads 05_transformers.joblib from disk, rebuilds a single test order through the loaded objects alone, and asserts the result matches what the notebook produced. This is the task's requirement that a production pipeline's output must match the notebook's — tested rather than assumed.

The leakage blocklist is a variable in the code, not a comment, and an assertion fails if any blocked column reaches the feature table. The scaler is fitted and saved but deliberately not applied: trees do not need it, and the stored tables stay readable.

Operationally: intervening on the riskiest 5% of orders catches 15% of the late ones on data the model has never seen — three times what random targeting would find. The reference figure from Task 1 (purchase-time features, a different label and split) was ROC-AUC 0.679.

On the gap between 0.774 and 0.711: ROC-AUC is a ranking metric and is not affected by the base rate, so this is a real loss of generalisation rather than an artefact. Training ends in March 2018 while the test window runs June to August — the model is predicting three to five months past anything it has seen. The operational conclusion is periodic retraining rather than a single deployment.

8. Two measured results that contradicted the prediction

8.1  The trees lost

Notebook 4 predicted tree models would win, for three stated reasons: the non-monotonic promise curve, heavy skew, and real interactions between columns. Logistic regression beat them on both metrics. The evidence for why is visible in the tuning itself — boosting performance degrades as rounds increase, with everything else held constant:

Validation ROC-AUC against boosting rounds — more training makes it worse

That is not undertraining; it is the model memorising the training period. Two further factors plausibly contribute: the target encodings had already turned the categorical signals into monotone numbers, leaving less for the trees to discover, and the moving base rate (7.95% → 5.53%) penalises flexible models more than constrained ones.

8.2  The feature that looked useless is the most important one

EVIDENCE 13 — permutation importance on validation
promised_days           0.1261
customer_state_te       0.0649
same_state              0.0436
distance_km             0.0255
main_seller_state_te    0.0202
...
promise_per_100km       0.0048

promised_days scored 0.487 on its own in the EDA — indistinguishable from noise. Inside the model it is the strongest feature by a wide margin, while promise_per_100km, the ratio the EDA built specifically to rescue it, is almost unused.

The explanation is that univariate strength is not multivariate importance. The promise's effect was masked by distance, with which it correlates 0.65. Once the model can see distance and promise as separate inputs, it can ask the right question itself — given this distance, is this promise enough? — and the effect appears. The hand-built ratio was necessary to see the phenomenon during analysis; it was not necessary to represent it inside a model that has both variables.

The remaining order matches the EDA exactly: customer state, then same-state, then distance. Geography governs the problem.

الارتباط الذي يفسّر ذلك: ارتباط الوعد بالمسافة 0.65 — الشركة تُطيل وعدها مع البُعد، فيلغي الأثران بعضهما عند النظر إلى الوعد منفردًا. والكمية المفيدة إذن ليست كم يومًا وُعِد بل كم يومًا وُعِد لكل ١٠٠ كم، أي هل الوعد سخيّ أم طموح نسبةً إلى الشحنة. ونتيجتها AUC = 0.612 — أقوى إشارة في البيانات.

٦. نوتبوك ٥ — الفيتشرز والكائنات التي يجب حفظها

الترميز المستهدَف بلا تسريب للّيبل: استبدال الولاية بنسبة تأخيرها التاريخية يخلق خطرين، وكلاهما عولج صراحةً:

  • الفئات الصغيرة تنتج ضجيجًا. ولاية فيها ١٢ طلبًا منها ٢ متأخران تقرأ ١٦.٧٪. التنعيم يسحب كل تقدير نحو المعدل العام بقوة ٥٠ مشاهدة وهمية، فتكاد الفئات الصغيرة لا تتحرّك عن المعدل بينما تحتفظ الكبيرة بنسبتها.
  • الصفوف كانت سترى ليبلها. ترميز صف تدريبي بمتوسط محسوب من مجموعة التدريب يُدخل ليبل ذلك الصف في ميزته. لذلك يُحسب الترميز داخل الـ train خارج الطيّة — خمس طيّات، كل واحدة تُرمَّز من الأربع الأخرى. أما الـ validation والـ test فيستعملان خريطة كامل الـ train، وهو ما سيستعمله الإنتاج تمامًا.

الخلية الأخيرة تعيد تحميل 05_transformers.joblib من القرص، وتبني طلبًا واحدًا من الـ test عبر الكائنات المحمَّلة وحدها، وتؤكّد تطابق الناتج مع ما أنتجه النوتبوك. وهذا هو شرط التاسك بأن تطابق مخرجات بايبلاين الإنتاج مخرجات النوتبوك — مُختبَرًا لا مفترضًا.

قائمة الحظر متغيّر في الكود لا تعليق، وتأكيد (assert) يفشل إن وصل أي عمود محظور إلى جدول الفيتشرز. والـ scaler مدرَّب ومحفوظ لكنه غير مطبَّق عمدًا: الأشجار لا تحتاجه، والجداول المحفوظة تبقى قابلة للقراءة.

٧. نوتبوك ٦ — النتائج

لا يُحمَّل ملف الـ test إلا في القسم الأخير، بعد تثبيت كل قرار.

٨. نتيجتان مقاستان خالفتا التوقّع

٨.١ الأشجار خسرت

توقّع نوتبوك ٤ فوز نماذج الأشجار لثلاثة أسباب مذكورة: منحنى الوعد غير الرتيب، والالتواء الشديد، والتفاعلات الحقيقية بين الأعمدة. وتفوّق عليها الانحدار اللوجستي في المقياسين. والدليل على السبب ظاهر في الضبط نفسه — أداء التعزيز يتراجع كلما زادت الجولات مع ثبات كل شيء آخر.

هذا ليس نقص تدريب بل حفظ للفترة التدريبية. وعاملان آخران يُرجَّح إسهامهما: الترميز المستهدَف كان قد حوّل الإشارات التصنيفية إلى أرقام رتيبة فلم يبقَ للأشجار كثير لتكتشفه، ومعدل الأساس المتحرّك (7.95٪ ← 5.53٪) يعاقب النماذج المرنة أكثر من المقيَّدة.
